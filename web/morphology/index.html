Honest, cross-validated results

What you're seeing. Real JWST/NIRCam cutouts (F200W+F356W+F444W of GOODS-S, COSMOS and UDS galaxies), pulled live from the DAWN JWST Archive cutout service. The labels are real human visual classifications — the canonical Galaxy Zoo: CANDELS split into featured/disk, smooth and merger (majority volunteer vote, ≥20 classifiers). Galaxy Zoo judged HST imaging; the ViT here is shown the JWST view of the same galaxies and learns morphology straight from the pixels. Next milestone: a self-supervised atlas of JWST galaxies you can fly through, and the hunt for the Little Red Dots.

Things to notice

It looks at the galaxy

Tick attention overlay: the transformer concentrates on the source and ignores the empty sky around it.

how that's drawn

Attention rollout

It chains the ViT's self-attention across all layers back to the image, showing which patches the network actually leaned on.

Nobody told it where the galaxy was — it learned to weight the structured pixels.

Honest about hard cases

In the confusion matrix, smooth and merger trip each other up far more than disks do.

why they blur

A real degeneracy

A relaxed elliptical and a settled merger remnant can look identical in a single image — even human classifiers disagree.

The model reports the confusion rather than hiding it; that's the honest 81% vs a 33% baseline.

Confidence tracks ambiguity

A clean disk scores ~99%; a faint or messy cutout splits its vote across the three classes.

what the bars mean

Not a coin flip

The bar heights are the softmax probabilities. A near-even split is the model honestly saying this one is genuinely ambiguous, not guessing.
